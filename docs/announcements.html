These letter ranges are approximate guides from the syllabus. They are not finalized course cutoffs or individual exam letter grades.

Performance varied substantially across the material. Students generally did well identifying sensory structures and describing the behaviors associated with jumping spider vision, moth bat-evasion, and passive electroreception. Cricket phonotaxis answers were more uneven, particularly when students had to connect recognition of a sound pattern with the behavioral response rather than simply define phonotaxis.

The greatest difficulty appeared in questions on motion detection, bat target-range computation, and developmental plasticity in owl sound localization. Many answers identified the relevant concept but did not explain the experiment, interpret the result, or establish why that result supported the proposed mechanism. Cross-species comparisons also lost points when answers described two animals separately without explaining the relevant similarity or difference.

As stated in the syllabus, knowing the terminology is the starting point. When a question asks you to explain a mechanism using evidence, naming a brain region or repeating a conclusion from a slide does not fully answer it. Strong responses identified what the researchers measured or manipulated, described what happened, and explained what could reasonably be concluded. Longer responses did not automatically receive more credit. Scientific accuracy and reasoning determined the score.

Seventy students scored below 60%. That is a substantial portion of the class, and those students should review their exams and seek help this week. A score below 60% on this exam does not automatically mean you have failed the course, but it does mean you need to assess your standing in the lecture component and change how you prepare.

The class average of 65.83% does not trigger an Exam 2 curve. Any curve will be applied at the end of the semester only if the final class average, calculated from the weighted lecture and laboratory grades before curving, is below 70%. You must still earn at least 60% in both components to pass. Do not plan your remaining work around an adjustment that may not occur.

For your review, return to each missed question and determine whether the problem was missing factual knowledge, misunderstanding a mechanism, misreading a figure, or failing to explain the evidence. Then practice answering that question again without your notes. Being able to recognize an explanation while reading it is different from being able to construct it yourself.

My office hours are Tuesdays and Thursdays from 5–7 pm in ANI 208. Bring specific questions and the lecture material you struggled with so we can identify where your reasoning breaks down.

Exam 3 is Monday, November 9, at 12:00 PM and covers Lectures 21 through 30. Under the one-week release schedule, recordings will begin becoming available on November 2, with remaining lectures added after they occur. Begin preparing now.

Two exams and the lecture project remain, together accounting for 60% of your lecture grade. There is still substantial work through which to demonstrate improvement, but that improvement needs to appear in the quality of your explanations and analysis.
