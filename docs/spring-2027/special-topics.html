Special topics

Beyond the 40 scheduled lectures, the course has a library of special-topic lectures on other animals and questions. They can replace a scheduled lecture during the semester, and every one of them is a reference for your lecture project and for going further.

Requesting a special topic

  • Throughout the semester, you may request that a special topic replace a scheduled lecture. A request can swap a lecture for the same subject in another animal (for example, a lecture on electroreception could be replaced with a special topic on electroreception and lateral-line sensing in the axolotl), or for any topic on the list below.
  • Special topics are not guaranteed. I decide whether a lecture is replaced, and usually only one to five lectures in a semester are replaced with special topics.
  • To make a request, e-mail jlopez11@usc.edu with the subject line “NEUR 411: Special topic request”. Name the topic you want, the scheduled lecture it would replace, and why.
  • Any replacement is announced in class and posted on the course calendar before the lecture.

Topics you can choose from (44 topics)

  • Marine and aquatic animals
  • Large mammals
  • Birds and reptiles
  • Social bonds, emotion and play
  • Brain plasticity, attention and sleep
  • Technology, virtual environments and biocomputing
  • Captivity, welfare and managed environments

Marine and aquatic animals (7)

  • Dolphins I: anatomy/neural pathways supporting echolocation & biosonar, auditory scene analysis during prey tracking and hunting strategies
  • Dolphins II: brain organization, signature whistles, vocal learning, and individual recognition in social communication and cognition
  • Killer whales: brain organization and auditory specializations, vocal traditions, social learning, and coordination of cooperative hunting
  • Seahorses: brain organization, visual processing, sensorimotor control of prey capture, courtship communication, and reproductive behavior
  • Axolotl: lateral-line sensing, electroreception, and sensory processing of courtship signals
  • Jellyfish I: neuroanatomy of Cassiopea, distributed nerve nets, rhopalia, and pacemaker control of rhythmic bell contractions
  • Jellyfish II: sleep-like states, sensory responsiveness, sleep homeostasis, and neural regulation of activity without a centralized brain

Large mammals (7)

  • Elephants: brain anatomy, auditory and somatosensory pathways, low-frequency hearing, seismic communication, and behavioral responses to ground-borne signals
  • Elephants II: Comparative Cognition, Vocal Learning, and Social Neurobiology
  • Elephants III: elephant Neuroethics, the Neurobiology of Captivity, and Behavioral Pathology
  • Giraffes: brain anatomy, visual processing, sensorimotor coordination, and control of head and neck movements
  • Rhinoceroses: brain/neuro anatomy & organization, olfactory and auditory systems, territorial signaling, and social recognition
  • Giant pandas: brain/neuro organization & anatomy, olfactory pathways, scent communication, and reproductive behavior
  • Pigs: brain & sensory neuroanatomy, olfactory exploration, learning and memory, and social cognition

Birds and reptiles (4)

  • Flamingos: balance and postural control, sensory guidance of feeding, and social communication
  • Songbirds I: Artificial song memories, optogenetic tutoring, and neural mechanisms of vocal imitation
  • Songbirds II: Closed-loop neural interfaces, dopamine reinforcement, and adaptive control of song
  • Turtles II: brain anatomy, sensory integration, spatial learning, and neural control of behavior

Social bonds, emotion and play (7)

  • Prairie voles I: partner preference, social attachment, and oxytocin–dopamine interactions during pair-bond formation
  • Prairie voles II: mate guarding, selective aggression, and neural mechanisms supporting the maintenance of established pair bonds
  • Prairie voles III: partner separation, stress signaling, and behavioral and nucleus accumbens adaptations following social loss
  • Substitute parents: decoys, robots, imprinting, surrogate caregivers, and cross-fostering across species
  • Tickling across species: play, positive affect, and laughter-like vocalizations in animals
  • Fear in dogs and cats: threat recognition, defensive behavior, and underlying neural circuits
  • Music and sound in animals: auditory perception, rhythm processing, and behavioral responses to music

Brain plasticity, attention and sleep (4)

  • Ferret: experimentally rewired sensory pathways, visual processing in auditory cortex, and developmental plasticity of cortical function
  • Hair cell regeneration: sensory repair in fish and birds, supporting-cell plasticity, and recovery of hearing and balance
  • Attention and impulsivity across species: comparative neural mechanisms of attentional control, dopamine and norepinephrine signaling, and animal models of ADHD
  • Unihemispheric Sleep in Animals: Comparative brain/neuro anatomy & organization, hemispheric specialization, predator vigilance, and the neural boundaries of consciousness

Technology, virtual environments and biocomputing (8)

  • Biocomputing I: Living neural networks, Yamanaka factors and cellular reprogramming, brain organoids, and biological learning — Cortical Labs, neural interfaces, and playing Pong
  • Biocomputing II: From Pong to Doom — sensory encoding, feedback, adaptive behavior, and neuroethical questions of sentience and welfare
  • Animals in Virtual and Interactive Environments I: Animals in virtual worlds, navigation, learning, and adaptive behavior across species
  • Animals in Virtual and Interactive Environments II: Animals at the controls, joysticks, touchscreens, vehicles, and virtual games
  • Zebrafish in virtual reality: brain-wide imaging, visual feedback, and neural mechanisms of motor adaptation
  • Goldfish behind the wheel: spatial cognition, visual guidance, and transfer of navigation skills across environments
  • Fish-Operated Vehicle II: Sensing, Control Mapping, and Constraints (Givon & Liu)
  • Computational Methods in Animal Research I: Welfare across species, Computer vision meets animal vision, tracking, virtual prey, and Designing games for animal minds, perception, agency, motivation

Captivity, welfare and managed environments (7)

  • Comparative Neuroethics: The Neurobiology of Captivity, Stress, and Behavioral Pathology Across Taxa
  • Animal behavior in aquariums, zoos, and theme parks: sensory environments, learning, stress, and enrichment
  • Animal Behavior in Managed Environments I: Zoos — sensory ecology, neural mechanisms of stress, social behavior, enrichment, and neuroethical considerations of captivity (also a Spring Recess field experience topic)
  • Animal Behavior in Managed Environments II: Aquariums & Marine Theme Parks — underwater sensory environments, learning and reward systems, social communication, and neuroethical considerations of training and performance (also a Spring Recess field experience topic)
  • Animal Behavior in Managed Environments III: Aquarium Sensory Worlds (visual, chemical, and mechanosensory processing, sensory adaptations, and how exhibit design shapes natural behavior) (also a Spring Recess field experience topic)
  • Animal Behavior in Managed Environments III: Rescue & Rehabilitation — stress, behavioral recovery, and readiness for release into the wild
  • Animal Behavior in Managed Environments IV: Rescue, Rehabilitation & Release (neurobiology of stress and injury, behavioral recovery, human habituation, and assessment of readiness for release) (also a Spring Recess field experience topic)
