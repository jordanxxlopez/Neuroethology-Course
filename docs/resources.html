Software for the computational work

The computational assignments and the simulation project use Python or MATLAB. Installation instructions are posted in Week 1; any of these options works.

  • Anaconda: Python with NumPy, SciPy, Matplotlib and Jupyter notebooks in one installer.
  • Python.org: the standard Python installer.
  • Google Colab: Python notebooks that run in the browser, with nothing to install. Sign in with your USC Google account.
  • MATLAB through USC Software: available to USC students through the university license.

Communication

Because this course involves complex, interdisciplinary concepts related to neural mechanisms and animal behavior, questions and clarification needs are expected. Students are strongly encouraged to use Teaching Assistants as their first point of contact for questions related to homework, labs, coding issues, and course logistics. TAs hold regular lab sessions and office hours and are well positioned to provide timely support. For questions that cannot be resolved with a TA, or for concerns related to unresolved grading disputes, accommodations, or broader course issues, students may contact the instructor at jlopez11@usc.edu.

I will make every effort to respond to emails and messages promptly; however, because I teach multiple courses and run an active research laboratory, response times may be delayed. Students should generally expect responses within one business day. Messages sent in the evening, overnight, or on weekends will typically be addressed the following business day. Use clear subject lines that include the course number and topic, such as “NEUR 411: Lab 3 report question.” Keep questions concise and include relevant context, such as the assignment name or lab section. Reach out early rather than waiting until deadlines or exam days. Consult the syllabus and current lecture materials for exam coverage before emailing.

Course Materials

There is no required textbook. Lecture slides are the primary source of instruction, and exams are based on lecture content. Recommended textbook: Zupanc, G. K. H. Behavioral Neurobiology: An Integrative Approach (3rd ed.). Oxford University Press, ISBN 978-0-19-873872-5. Alternative: Carew, T. J. (2000). Behavioral Neurobiology: The Cellular Organization of Natural Behavior. Sinauer.

Readings are listed in the Readings Calendar after the Course Calendar. They are recommended, not required. I will teach the material in lecture, and the readings are provided so that you can go deeper and succeed on exams.

Required equipment: a laptop capable of running Python or MATLAB for the simulation project, and a reliable connection to the course site. Software is free to install; instructions will be posted in Week 1.

Lecture Recordings and Study Materials

Lectures are recorded. Recordings are released one week before the exam that covers them. I may release them two weeks before an exam at my discretion, but early release is not guaranteed and should not be expected. You are responsible for attending lecture, taking thorough notes, and studying throughout the semester. Do not wait for recordings to begin preparing for an exam.

Recordings from previous offerings may also be posted as supplemental study materials. This course changes between semesters, and older recordings may omit current topics or include material that is no longer covered. This semester’s lectures determine what is examinable. You are responsible for identifying those differences using the current lectures and slides.

Recordings do not replace attendance or excuse arriving unprepared. Lecture content is dense and requires consistent engagement. Choosing to rely on recordings does not reduce the material you are expected to know or change the standards by which your work will be graded.

Study Expectations

This course covers a lot of material on a fast schedule, and it will punish procrastination. Plan at least two hours of outside study for every hour of class. Review each lecture within 48 hours. Read the recommended papers for each lecture in the Readings Calendar. Work through the figures until you can explain the data without looking at the caption. Form study groups but make sure each of you can answer exam questions alone. Do not wait until the week of an exam to begin studying, and do not start the project the week it is due.
